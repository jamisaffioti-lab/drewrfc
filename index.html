History
The DRFC Story
1963
Drew Rugby Football Club is formed as a men’s rugby team at Drew University in Madison, NJ — planting the roots of what would grow into one of the northeast’s most respected programs.
1971
DRFC becomes an officially recognized student club at Drew University and joins full USA Rugby Union intercollegiate competition, traveling to matches and tournaments across the Atlantic Seaboard.
Peak Era
DRFC reaches its competitive peak, defeating D-I opponents and earning national recognition despite competing at the D-III level. The club becomes a powerhouse of northeast collegiate rugby.
Legacy
The club ceases operations, but the alumni network remains active and proud. Efforts are now underway to bring rugby back to Drew University and restore the club to its former glory.

Hall of Fame
Inductees

Honoring the players who defined Drew Rugby Football Club.

Keith Martin
Prop · 8-Man · Class of ’80
DRFC HoF 2026

Keith Martin attended Drew University from 1976 through 1980, majoring in History and Political Science. His introduction to rugby came on his very first day at Drew, when Warren Paul C’78 invited him into the UC Pub, introduced him to the upper-class rugby players, and convinced him to come to practice the very next day — without a uniform or equipment. He was put in at A side prop and learned the game on the fly.

Keith played prop and wing forward his first two years before studying in London in the fall of his junior year, where he played for the Sudbury North Wembley Wasps and received extensive training at the 8-man position. Upon returning to Drew, he applied those skills alongside his talented teammates to accumulate impressive statistics over his final two seasons.

The most memorable season for Keith was the 1978–1979 team, which shut out every college team they faced. After Drew, Keith went on to Delaware Law School and has been a practicing attorney in Philadelphia since 1983. He is honored to share this induction with his wife, Anne.

Dan Boyer
Fullback · Class of ’69 · P’04
DRFC HoF 2026

Dan Boyer came to Drew in 1965 knowing nothing about rugby other than it was played with a “fat football.” He attended his first practice in spring 1966 and quickly became a committed rugger, his development shaped largely by fellow inductee John Hinchcliff (DRFC HoF 2022), who taught the young team how to play the game the New Zealand way.

After playing various positions, Dan settled at fullback for most of his Drew career. A standout moment came at the Thanksgiving Sevens Tournament on Randalls Island (NYC) in 1967, where DRFC — with no sevens experience — surprised opponents and won three games to reach the quarterfinals. Dan and Dwight Davies (DRFC HoF 2024) were elected co-captains for the 1968–69 season, and their capstone achievement was a win over Princeton in spring 1969 to capture the Schaeffer Cup for the first time.

Dan continued playing at Syracuse during grad school, returned to the pitch in 1975 while working at Drew, and played several more seasons before hanging up his boots. The friendships forged with his teammates are what have endured most to this day.

Eric Kaeuper
Prop · Class of ’03
DRFC HoF 2026

Eric Kaeuper’s introduction to rugby came in middle school in Denver, Colorado, when an Australian teacher gifted him a video of the 1995 Rugby World Cup final — a moment that sparked a lifelong fascination with the sport. He arrived at Drew intending to play baseball for the Rangers, but after not making the team he found his path with the Drew rugby squad. Starting as a lock, Eric transitioned to the front row, playing both tighthead and loosehead prop during his final two years.

After graduating, Eric took a hiatus until fellow Drew alumni Ryan Meiller C’05 and Michael Wieners C’04 recruited him to the Princeton Athletic Club Rugby. He declared himself a back and moved to center, showcasing versatility by playing all along the back row for both A and B sides. With PAC 7s he returned to the front row, playing prop while handling much of the kicking duties, before retiring from competitive rugby in 2010.

Eric now resides in Garwood, New Jersey, with his wife Heather and her two now-grown children, Brooke and Ethan.

Rebecca Kuhar
Wing · Class of ’96 · DWRFC
DRFC HoF 2026

Rebecca “Happy Hour” (Morris) Kuhar arrived on the Drew campus in 1992 and was quickly attracted to the nascent Drew Women’s Football Club, formed in the spring of 1991. She played all four years, almost always at outside or wing, and scored one of the team’s early tries — a memorable run up the outside of the field that may or may not have been contested, but which she recalls vividly to this day.

Rebecca has remained deeply engaged with the DWRFC, supporting the 2025 annual game and enlisting current and recent members of the women’s team to keep the tradition alive — including organizing a successful co-ed touch game in 2025 when the men’s team couldn’t field enough players on their own.

Off the pitch, Rebecca majored in economics with a business management minor and received her Master of Public Administration from Columbia University’s SIPA. She lives in Midtown Manhattan and works at J.P. Morgan. She serves on the Drew University College Alumni Association Board and mentors Drew students on the Wall Street Semester, crediting her formative years at Drew — including semesters abroad in Egypt and Brussels — as instrumental to her career in public policy.

Harry Litwack
Head Prop · Kicker
Inducted 2023 · Class of ’71

Harry Litwack graduated from Drew University in 1971 with a BA, majoring in Sociology with an Anthropology minor, and went on to earn his MA in School Psychology while studying Educational Administration at Rowan University, receiving New Jersey teaching and Principal/Supervisor certifications. Harry also has a certificate in Filmmaking from New York University and has had two careers: education and film production. The mentoring skills he gained at Drew on the rugby pitch and in classrooms have been a lifelong guiding force throughout his life and career.

Arriving at Drew from Vineland, NJ, in the fall of 1967, he saw his first rugby match, thinking “What the hell are these guys doing and why?” He SOON found out in the spring of 1968, putting aside basketball and baseball to learn a new sport — RUGBY. His freshman year roommate, John Marinaro, convinced him to try out for the rugby team. After Drew, John was one of the founders of the Morris County Rugby Club. In 1971, John’s cousin, Ed Marinaro, was runner-up for the Heisman Trophy as a running back at Cornell.

As an A team player, Harry went from tight head prop to loosehead prop and kicker as a sophomore, to fly half his junior year, to wing forward as he was needed his Senior year. He followed the kicking tradition of John Hinchcliffe and Don Clarke and took pride in learning rugby, defending Drew, teaching, and mentoring the illustrious history of the DRFC. Harry would like to thank ALL his teammates and Alums he has gotten to know over six decades. Ball coming in NOW.

Bob Ficalora
Fly-Half · Fly Captain
Inducted 2023 · Class of ’79

Bob Ficalora C’79 arrived at Drew in the fall of 1975 and joined the rugby club in the fall of 1976. Bob had an immediate impact both on and off the pitch. As a player, Fic brought a mix of passing, kicking, and running experience, as well as tenacious defense, to the fly-half position, which greatly improved the team’s performance. Along with Mike Smith, Fic captained the highly successful 1978–1979 seasons, with 1979 being one the most successful in Drew Rugby history.

Bob had as much value off the pitch as on. Through innovative fund-raising and recruitment of many younger players, his legacy lasted long after his playing days. After leaving Drew, Bob married Helen (Schneiderman) Ficalora, C’82, and had a long career with IBM. Bob an Helen, who live in Florida, have two sons, Lennon and Marley, and three grandchildren, granddaughter Montauk, and grandsons Sonny and Malibu.

F. Bock Moyes
Fly Half · Captain
Inducted 2023 · Class of ’92

Frank Bockius Moyes (Bock) started playing rugby at the Down’s School in England at age eight and continued playing at Bedford School. He also played for the Midlands U18 team and was probably on his way to playing for England, except that he was too small and too slow. So he took the road less traveled, enrolled at Drew University in the Fall of 1988.

As one of the only club members with ingrained rugby DNA, he was elected a team captain in his inaugural season and had an immediate impact as starting scrum half. His natural position was Fly Half, but Senior Joe Somerville was not giving that up! They were a dynamic duo that first season. The club that made a significant impact, including Eric Stofman, Paul Skilton, Dathe Grey, Sam Hijab, Marco Cavallaro, and Greg Gordon (the latter mostly as beer-meister).

Bock captained the team for the entirety of his career at Drew from 1988–1992, starting at Fly Half of Sophomore through his Senior year. Although statistics were not consistently recorded in those ancient days, we are pretty sure he led the team in tries, missed penalty kicks, and broken noses. He was also consistently selected for and started on the NY Met Union college select side.

Emily Riggs Fennessey
Wing · DWRFC
Inducted 2023 · Class of ’96

Emily “Riggsy” Riggs Fennessey arrived on the Drew Campus in 1992 and was quickly attracted to the nascent Drew Women’s Football Club. She played all four years — almost always at the wing. Who would have thought the team’s first try wouldn’t happen until the Fall of 1995 — or that Riggsy would be the one to score it? The ball didn’t get out cleanly to the wing all that often in the early years. But that is what happened: Emily recalls — all Emily recalls — is about that momentous event, that she had the ball within a maul and rode her teammates into the try zone, where she touched it down. It remains to be confirmed whether the conversion was successful.

While that try was a highlight of her Drew rugby career, the friendships she had among her teammates are what she remembers most fondly, and they have endured to this day. When not playing rugby, Emily majored in psych and minored in studio art. She received her Master’s in Social Work in 2000 from Simmons University in Boston. Emily lives in Ridgefield, CT, with her husband Bob, and three children Amelia (19), Caraline (16), and James (14). She currently works as an LMSW for Benchmark Infant and Toddler Services.

Bill Bernhard
Fullback · Class of ’82 · 3× MVP · USA Eagles
DRFC HoF 2022

Bill Bernhard played Fullback for Drew Rugby from 1978 to 1981, earning MVP in 1979, 1980, and 1981, and serving as team captain in 1981. In March 1982, he toured England and Ireland with Montclair “Face-Crushers” RFC.

From 1982 to 1984, Bill played for the Long Island RFC, serving as team captain in 1983, before joining the Washington Rugby Club until 1990 where he was MVP in 1985 and helped make WRFC Potomac Union Rugby Champions from 1984–1990. He earned selection to the Potomac Rugby Union Select Side and Eastern Rugby Union Select Side as Fullback from 1985–1989.

Bill reached the pinnacle of the sport when he was selected for the USA Eagles in February 1987. He was unfortunately injured and unable to compete for the Rugby World Cup Team in Australia, but returned to represent the Atlantis Select Side 7’s Team on tour in Scotland in fall 1988.

Don Clarke
Number 8 · Class of ’72 · Potomac Union Select Side
DRFC HoF 2022

Don Clarke played every Drew A game from his first semester in fall 1965 through spring 1968, before joining the Army and Helicopter Flight School, serving one tour in Vietnam as a Cobra Pilot in the Air Cavalry. He re-enrolled at Drew in January 1971 and played every A game through his graduation with a BA in Political Science in spring 1972.

In 1974 Don co-founded the Princeton Athletic Club, playing A side #8 through 1975. He then moved to Washington, DC and played #8 for Sud Americano A’s from 1975–1980, earning selection to the Potomac Rugby Union Select Side as #8 in 1979. He retired from the pitch in 1980, ran marathons for three years, and returned for one final season with the LA Rugby Club “Lunchtime Legends” old boys team in 1990.

With the exception of his first two years at Drew — where fellow inductee John Hinchcliff held the #8 position — Don always played #8 and took all penalty kicks. He was likely the leading scorer at Drew during his nine seasons and the second-leading scorer at Princeton Athletic Club.

John Hinchcliff
Loose Forward · Class of ’69 · New Zealand
DRFC HoF 2022

John Hinchcliff began playing rugby as a nine-year-old in New Zealand. At Nelson College his commitment deepened, and at Canterbury University (1959–61) he played mostly loose forward at one of New Zealand’s finest programs.

A Rotary scholarship brought John to Israel, and later a Doctorate program brought him to Drew — which he described as a “pleasant, friendly and caring campus.” His memories of Drew rugby center on a group of committed players who became a strong platform to develop a fine team. John brought with him the rugby culture and traditions of New Zealand, helping shape the identity of the early DRFC.

After Drew, John taught Philosophy at Hampden-Sydney College in Virginia, where he found a similarly small and dedicated group of rugby enthusiasts. He remains an admirer of the game from, as he puts it, “the comfort of my armchair.”
